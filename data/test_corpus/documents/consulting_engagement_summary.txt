1. Client Profile

Meridian Aerospace Holdings plc is a mid-cap aerospace components manufacturer
headquartered in Bristol, United Kingdom. The company employs approximately
1,400 staff across three production sites (Bristol, Toulouse, and Hamburg)
and generated GBP 185M in revenue in FY2023. Core business segments include
precision turbine components (62% of revenue) and avionics housing systems (31%).

2. Engagement Scope

Meridian engaged our firm to support the implementation of a unified Manufacturing
Execution System (MES) across all three sites. The current landscape consists of
three separate legacy systems:
- Bristol: Siemens Opcenter (formerly Camstar), installed 2016
- Toulouse: Dassault DELMIA, installed 2019
- Hamburg: Custom SAP ME extension, maintained by interne IT since 2014

The target architecture consolidates on PTC ThingWorx as the central IoT platform,
with Rockwell FactoryTalk as the shop-floor execution layer. Integration with the
existing Windchill PLM environment is a critical requirement.

3. Key Findings

a) Process Maturity Assessment
   - Bristol site operates at CMMI Level 3 (Defined); Toulouse at Level 2 (Managed);
     Hamburg at Level 1 (Initial). Harmonization to Level 3 across all sites is
     the minimum viable target.
   - The proprietary "AeroTrack" quality tracking system at the Bristol site handles
     approximately 23,000 inspection records per month. Migration to the unified
     MES must preserve full traceability for AS9100 compliance.

b) Technology Gaps
   - Hamburg's SAP ME extension lacks real-time OEE monitoring. Current OEE is
     calculated manually with a 48-hour delay.
   - No site currently supports digital twin integration. Meridian's competitor,
     RollsTech Precision Ltd., has deployed digital twins for 40% of their
     production lines, creating competitive pressure.

c) Organizational Readiness
   - The Head of Digital Manufacturing, Dr. Amir Patel, reports to the Bristol
     CTO rather than the Group COO, creating misaligned incentives for cross-site
     standardization.
   - Toulouse site management (led by Marie-Claire Dubois, Directrice de Production)
     has expressed concerns about losing autonomy over production scheduling.

5. Risk Register (Top 3)

R1: Toulouse Works Council Approval
    The Comité Social et Économique (CSE) must approve the MES transition under
    French labor law. Timeline risk: 6-8 weeks for consultation process.
    Mitigation: Early engagement via Marie-Claire Dubois, scheduled for October 2024.

R2: AS9100 Re-certification
    Changing the quality tracking system triggers a mandatory re-certification
    audit. Estimated cost: GBP 45,000. Must be completed by March 2025.

R3: Vendor Lock-in
    PTC ThingWorx licensing terms include a 5-year minimum commitment.
    Alternative evaluated and rejected: Siemens MindSphere (higher latency
    for shop-floor integration, 340ms vs. 85ms p95).

6. Recommendations

- Proceed with phased rollout: Bristol (Q1 2025), Hamburg (Q3 2025), Toulouse (Q1 2026)
- Appoint a Group-level Digital Manufacturing Director reporting to COO
- Establish a cross-site Center of Excellence for MES operations in Bristol
- Budget an additional GBP 180,000 for change management and training

7. Lessons Learned

The failed attempt to implement a unified ERP system in 2021 (Project ATLAS,
abandoned after 14 months and GBP 1.2M spend) demonstrates that cross-site
technology programs require executive sponsorship at Group level, not site level.
This engagement has that sponsorship through the CEO, Dame Patricia Whitfield,
but sustaining it through the 18-month implementation will require structured
governance.
